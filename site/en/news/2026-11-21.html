01

US stocks plunge as AI selloff pushes S&P 500 into correction

On Friday, November 20, a steep sell-off in AI shares sent the S&P 500 down 3.7% to 6,910.39, its worst week in more than 18 months (down 8.6% for the week). The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% to 22,617.73 and the Dow lost 1.9% to 48,147.33. The S&P 500 is now more than 10% below its October 6 record, meaning it has entered a correction.

Anthropic prices IPO at $83, then fades toward offer price

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on November 18, above its initial $70–$78 range, valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion. Shares began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on November 19, opening at $111.5 and closing at $97.17. By November 20, Fortune reported the stock had slipped to just 5 cents above its IPO price.

Nvidia faces SEC probe after CEO Jensen Huang resigns

The Wall Street Journal reported the SEC has opened a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, including questions around chip sales in Southeast Asia and China. Huang resigned as CEO over multiple extramarital relationships, and Nvidia shares have fallen more than 10% since his exit. California's attorney general says his office is assessing the matter.

04

Nvidia lifts revenue outlook to about 75% growth for fiscal 2028

Interim CEO Colette Kress told analysts after Nvidia's November 17 earnings that revenue for the fiscal year ending 2028 should grow roughly 75% year over year, up from the 70% forecast given in August. Shares recovered to around $160 in after-hours trading, though leadership uncertainty continued to weigh on the stock.

05

Hot US inflation and hawkish Fed talk lift December hike bets

US CPI rose 0.4% month on month, above the 0.3% expected, and Fed Governor Waller raised the prospect of a 50-basis-point hike in December. As of November 12, fed funds futures were pricing about 29 basis points of tightening by December, with roughly a 16% probability assigned to a 50bp move, according to StoneX.

12

Yen nears 160 per dollar as Japan warns of intervention

The dollar hit 159.94 yen on November 12, its strongest in over two months, as expectations of Fed hikes pushed the greenback higher. Currency diplomat Atsushi Mimura said it was 'too early to forget' the summer's yen-buying interventions and that recent moves were excessively rapid. Japan's yen-based import price index rose 24.8% in October from a year earlier.

13

BOJ Governor Ueda says rate hikes should continue

Governor Kazuo Ueda told parliament on November 18 that the central bank should keep raising its policy rate while anchoring underlying inflation around 2%, arguing financial conditions remain accommodative. The policy rate stands at 1.25% after the BOJ held at its October 30 meeting. He also said a planned cut to the food consumption tax could support household purchasing power.

SourcesReuters（via New Straits Times） ↗
14

ECB holds at 2.5% as Lagarde announces early exit

The European Central Bank kept its deposit rate at 2.5% on October 29, the same day Christine Lagarde said she will leave on February 7, 2027, about nine months before her term ends. Eurozone inflation was 3.8% in September, nearly double the 2% target. French 10-year yields rose above 5%, with ING saying the bond-market move had done part of the ECB's tightening work.

SourcesEuronews ↗
15

Oil swings on uncertain Iran war outlook; Brent near $93

As of November 11, Brent was near $93 and WTI above $86, per Bloomberg, after both retreated from six-month highs on hopes the US–Iran conflict will ease, though talks have shown little progress. Saudi crude output rose to 10.56 million barrels a day in October, adding supply, and the EIA raised its 2027 retail gasoline forecast to $3.73 a gallon.
